Bibigo Index

Reading the world economy through 100g of dumplings

In Korea, 100g of Bibigo Wang Gyoza costs 1,093.33 won. What does it cost in grocery baskets around the world? Local sale prices are matched to the same weight, and relative prices and exchange rates by country are examined alongside the Big Mac.

01 / WHY

Why it was made

The Economist's Big Mac Index is the best-known indicator for intuitively comparing prices and exchange rates across countries. But Korean readers are more familiar with Korean brands. So we tested whether the same comparison could be made with Bibigo Wang Gyoza dumplings.

The local sale price of Bibigo dumplings is standardized to the same 100g, and relative prices with the Korean price set to 100 are compared by country. Paired with the Big Mac Index, it also shows how similar the conclusions of the two indices are.

The service states that products with a different taste or composition are excluded from highest and lowest judgments, and that it does not assert a representative price for any country. This index is an exploratory comparison, not an academic purchasing power parity (PPP) index.

03 / DATA & METHODS

Data and analysis method

Bibigo dumpling sale prices
45 entries from 36 countries. Retailer sources are disclosed, prices are standardized to 100g, and verification status is distinguished. Includes retailer and supporting sources and data that needs checking.
Exchange rates
Common exchange-rate reference date 2026-10-16, KRW per 1 unit of local currency.
Big Mac prices
Used for the paired comparison with the Big Mac Index. The 8 countries that have both indices and a common exchange rate are the default paired-comparison sample.

Data checked 2026-10-17. The index is a sample result using only countries whose products are similar in composition to the reference product and whose common exchange rate was confirmed. Local-currency price, package weight and source status must be read together.

04 / HOW TO USE

How it can be used

Economics education and content

Explains the concepts of purchasing power parity and the Big Mac Index intuitively with a Korean-brand product.

Economy and prices reporters

Use relative prices of the same product by country as a story angle, while noting the sample limits the service discloses.

General readers

Take a light look at how different overseas grocery prices are from Korea's.
