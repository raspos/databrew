01 / WHY

Why we built it

Marriage ties between families are often mentioned when explaining corporate governance and succession of control, but they are buried in scattered reports and disclosures and are hard to see at a glance.

We made corporate families the nodes and verified marriages the lines, so you can see on a map how each family connects to which business group.

The service states that it includes only relationships cross-checked against disclosures and Fair Trade Commission data. Family trees are built around the first to fourth generations and the executives within the scope of public data.

02 / FEATURES

What you can do

Chaebol marriage-tie map

Zoom and pan the full network drawn with families as nodes and marriage ties as lines, and save it as an image.

Marriage-tie matching by company

Pick a company and it finds the families and marriage ties connected to it.

Lineage of corporate splits

Follow the lineages of companies that split off and became independent from a group, and see 11 cases of independence.

Network analysis from many angles

Analyze connected families and business groups from several perspectives.

Detailed family tree by family

Pick a family to see its first-to-fourth-generation family tree and its executives.

Search and share

Search people, families, and companies, and share the current view as a link.

03 / DATA & METHODS

Data and analysis methods

Financial Supervisory Service DART
Disclosure data such as executive status. Used to confirm links between people and companies.
Fair Trade Commission
Data on large business groups designated for disclosure. The basis for the 40 connected business groups.
Relationship verification
A relationship verification rate of 100%, as stated by the service. Includes 58 marriage ties that passed cross-checking against independent sources and 11 independent split-offs.

Figures are the values shown on the service screen (data as of 2026-10-25). The verification criteria and methods are in the service's Verification and Methodology tab.
