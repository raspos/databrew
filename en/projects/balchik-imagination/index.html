Bold Imagination

What if Korea and Japan became one country?

Between adding two countries together and becoming one. An interactive thought experiment in which you add up the idea of a Korea–Japan community in numbers, weigh everyday differences, public sentiment and the EU's experience, and then design the community's rules yourself.

01 / WHY

Why it was made

The idea of a Korea–Japan community resurfaces from time to time, with politicians speaking of security and businesspeople of markets. The service takes as its starting point remarks by former Japanese Prime Minister Shigeru Ishiba and SK Group and Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry chairman Chey Tae-won.

But adding up the scale of two countries and becoming one country are entirely different matters. Numbers add up, but history and sovereignty do not add up that way. This thought experiment was built to let you handle that gap directly with data.

The service holds to the principle "the question is bold, the numbers are strict." The outlooks in the text are editorial analysis based on data and institutions, and are not predictions of the probability of realization or the timing of adoption.

02 / FEATURES

What you can do

Combined, what rank in the world

Simply adds nominal GDP, population, military spending and manufacturing value added to compare world rankings. You can also check each country's rank and base year directly.

Everyday Korea–Japan differences

Compares one hour of minimum wage, the same car, an 84㎡ home in Seoul and Tokyo, and the basic subway fare on the same won basis.

How many dollars is one unit of the new currency

Set the weights and units of the won and yen yourself and calculate how many dollars a hypothetical new currency would be worth. It is a hypothetical conversion, not a market exchange-rate forecast.

Thirteen years of public sentiment

See favorability toward each other and public opinion for and against an economic community in year-by-year graphs.

The EU's experience

A timeline from the 1950 Schuman Declaration to Brexit shows how far the EU has become one, and considers the implications for Korea and Japan.

Community design room

Choose decision rules such as level of integration, a joint parliament and a common currency yourself, and design your own community.

03 / DATA & METHODS

Data and analysis method

Scale comparison
Nominal GDP from the IMF (2024), population from the UN medium projection (2024), military spending from SIPRI (2025), manufacturing value added from World Bank and BEA (2024). Totals are simple sums, and the base year differs by indicator.
Everyday comparison
The 2026 minimum wage in effect, vehicle list prices, Seoul and Tokyo housing prices, subway fares and more. Conversion to won uses the ECB exchange rate of 2026-10-30 and does not include exchange fees.
Public sentiment
EAI and IHJ surveys. In 2025 the partner institutions and survey system changed, so long-term changes may be mixed with the effect of the change in method.

The service states that it uses static data collected in advance, with data checked 2026-10-31, and makes no LLM calls on visitors' behalf. Sources, calculation methods and JSON downloads can be found in the service's data and methodology section. Military spending rank is not the same as actual defense capability, and housing and car comparisons may not be like-for-like products.
